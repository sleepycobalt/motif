## I-01 — Metadata cannot substitute for lived context in archived qualitative data
**Claim:** Researchers across criminal justice, management accounting, and heritage studies argue that structured metadata cannot capture the situational, organizational, and political context that gave qualitative data its meaning, making later reuse epistemically risky.  
**Confidence:** medium  
**Sources:** bruce, michelle, stephen  
**Evidence:** michelle:0028, stephen:0022, bruce:0035  
**Counter-evidence:** bruce:0035, david:0040 — Bruce remains genuinely undecided about whether missing context is a real problem, while David -- whose poststructural/new-materialist framework and early-career, non-academic-background profile differ most from the other participants -- explicitly reframes decontextualized reuse as generative rather than epistemically risky, showing this is a live disagreement rather than consensus.  
**Opportunity:** Build a mandatory structured 'context companion' (beyond checkbox metadata) into repository deposit workflows -- a narrative field prompting depositors to describe organizational, political, and temporal circumstances at time of collection.

**Opportunity:** Develop a 'compliance-safe' deposit tier specifically for commercially-sensitive organizational research -- depositing only de-identified thematic codebooks, methodological workings, and aggregated findings (as Stephen already does in publication) rather than raw transcripts, formalized as an accepted archival category.
