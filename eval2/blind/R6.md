## I-01 — Context loss (metadata or tacit knowledge) risks shallow secondary understanding
**Claim:** Two researchers argue that the political, funding, organizational, and tacit context surrounding qualitative data cannot be fully conveyed to secondary users — whether attempted through metadata or held only as the original researcher's subjective, undocumented understanding — meaning secondary analysis risks being shallow or 'sterile'.  
**Confidence:** medium  
**Sources:** michelle, stephen  
**Evidence:** michelle:0032, stephen:0022  
**Counter-evidence:** david:0012 — David reframes the same loss-of-context risk as a productive feature, arguing new readers can spark genuinely new research rather than only misreading it.  
**Opportunity:** Repository deposit templates should include a mandatory free-text 'situating narrative' field (funding climate, organizational change, timing, researcher's undocumented contextual knowledge) alongside structured metadata, not just demographic tags.

## I-04 — Confidentiality to commissioning organizations is a strong but not always absolute barrier
**Claim:** In commissioned research (corporate case studies, agency contracts), confidentiality obligations to the organizations that grant access are treated as a strong default barrier to sharing raw data, though the objection is often specifically about revealing an organization's name rather than all disclosure, and can be renegotiated in some commissioned contexts.  
**Confidence:** medium  
**Sources:** penni, stephen  
**Evidence:** stephen:0018, stephen:0047, penni:0031  
**Counter-evidence:** stephen:0038, penni:0040 — Stephen's own account shows organizational objections are often narrowly about naming rather than substantive disclosure, and Penni's public-inquiry project shows a commissioning context can still yield an open-data mandate when transparency is core to the commissioning body's mission — complicating the 'hard, all-or-nothing barrier' framing.  
**Opportunity:** Develop standard tiered disclosure agreements with commissioning organizations at project inception (e.g., theoretical/methodological findings open, organization-identifying detail withheld) rather than treating openness as all-or-nothing.
