## I-01 — Genuine technical anonymization is difficult even with rigorous de-identification
**Claim:** Bruce and Penni both report that full technical anonymization of qualitative data is often unattainable in practice, since small stakeholder pools or key informants can remain identifiable even after standard de-identification steps.  
**Confidence:** medium  
**Sources:** bruce, penni  
**Evidence:** bruce:0023, penni:0031  
**Counter-evidence:** bruce:0033 — Bruce's own multilayered de-identification practice on another project shows functional anonymization can be achieved with enough time and process, suggesting the barrier is partly resourcing-dependent rather than absolute for all datasets.  
**Opportunity:** Fund dedicated, tiered anonymization support (specialist staff time, standard de-identification protocols) as a costed line item in grants and agency contracts, with explicit guidance on residual re-identification risk for small stakeholder pools.

## I-04 — Reproducibility/replicability norms borrowed from quantitative science are seen by academics as ill-fitting for qualitative openness, but this is contested
**Claim:** Bruce, David, and Michelle resist applying quantitative quality markers (reproducibility, inter-rater agreement) to qualitative open data, arguing qualitative research needs its own definition of quality and transparency.  
**Confidence:** medium  
**Sources:** bruce, david, michelle  
**Evidence:** michelle:0101, bruce:0021, david:0058  
**Counter-evidence:** penni:0031, penni:0083 — Penni, the corpus's only agency-based researcher, takes the opposite position: she frames qualitative research's comparative lack of transparency and validation as a genuine weakness relative to other methods, and welcomes theoretical frameworks precisely because they make analytic decisions auditable in a quantitative-adjacent way.  
**Opportunity:** Funders and repositories should publish qualitative-specific quality criteria (process transparency, audit trail, positionality statements) as an explicit alternative standard for qualitative deposits, while also engaging practitioners like Penni who want more, not less, alignment with transparency/validation norms.
