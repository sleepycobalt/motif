## I-01 — Metadata cannot substitute for lived context, so shared qualitative data risks being misread
**Claim:** Researchers in criminal justice (Michelle) and management accounting (Stephen) argue that no amount of standardized metadata can convey the temporal, organizational, and relational context in which qualitative data was produced, making secondary interpretation inherently partial or 'sterile.'  
**Confidence:** medium  
**Sources:** michelle, stephen  
**Evidence:** michelle:0028, michelle:0036, stephen:0022  
**Counter-evidence:** david:0040 — David reframes context-loss as generative reuse rather than degradation, showing the barrier is philosophically contested, not universally accepted.  
**Opportunity:** Repositories should support (and funders should budget for) rich, structured 'context companions'—not just field-level metadata but narrative notes on organizational, political and temporal circumstances—co-authored by the depositing researcher.

## I-02 — Confidentiality obligations to organizations, and separately to vulnerable individuals, are treated as a near-absolute block on sharing
**Claim:** Researchers describe two related but distinct confidentiality barriers: commercial/organizational non-disclosure agreements that require legal sign-off before any data can be shared, and protective duties to politically vulnerable or at-risk individual participants; both are frequently described as preconditions for data collection that are incompatible with open deposit.  
**Confidence:** medium  
**Sources:** bruce, penni, stephen  
**Evidence:** stephen:0018, stephen:0038, bruce:0023, penni:0031  
**Counter-evidence:** david:0036 — David shows the barrier can be softened through negotiated, tiered consent rather than being treated as absolute.  
**Opportunity:** Develop two distinct templates: a commercial-sensitivity clearance workflow (organizational sign-off, disguised-industry reporting) for cases like Stephen's, and a separate tiered/negotiated consent template (closed/tailored/open, as David uses) for vulnerable-population research like Penni's and Bruce's, rather than treating 'confidentiality' as a single undifferentiated barrier.

## I-03 — Anonymization is understood to be leaky, not a solved problem
**Claim:** Researchers across sectors report that removing names and places does not reliably prevent re-identification, especially for small, distinctive, or locally-known populations, undermining confidence that de-identification makes open sharing safe.  
**Confidence:** medium  
**Sources:** bruce, penni, stephen  
**Evidence:** bruce:0023, bruce:0027, stephen:0032, penni:0031  
**Counter-evidence:** none  
**Opportunity:** Provide risk-assessment tooling (a 'small-N/distinctiveness' checklist) so researchers can systematically decide whether a given transcript is safely shareable rather than relying on ad hoc judgment.
