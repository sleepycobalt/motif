**Confidence:** medium  
**Sources:** bruce, penni, stephen  
**Evidence:** penni:0031, bruce:0023, stephen:0032  
**Counter-evidence:** bruce:0033 — Bruce shows that with enough time-intensive de-identification work (removing all place/organisation references), archiving can proceed, suggesting the barrier is cost/effort rather than absolute impossibility.  
**Opportunity:** Develop tiered disclosure/access models (e.g. UKDS-style restricted/safeguarded access with re-identification risk assessment) rather than a binary open/closed anonymisation standard, so repositories can accept incompletely-anonymisable data under controlled access.

## I-03 — Consent obtained at collection cannot meaningfully cover unknown future data uses
**Claim:** Researchers identify a structural problem in informed consent for open data: participants agree to future archiving without knowing when, how, or by whom their data will eventually be used, which researchers themselves cannot specify at consent time.  
**Confidence:** medium  
**Sources:** michelle, penni  
**Evidence:** michelle:0054, penni:0040  
**Counter-evidence:** david:0036, david:0030 — David (whose method and consent practice differ sharply from Michelle's and Penni's) describes an explicit tailored-consent 'menu' negotiated per participant plus a policy of reminding participants of intended data uses at every subsequent contact, suggesting the problem can be partially managed through dynamic, ongoing consent rather than being fixed and unresolvable at a single collection-time checkbox.  
**Opportunity:** Design layered/dynamic consent tools (e.g. re-contact triggers or participant dashboards, following David's tailored-menu and repeated-reminder model) that let participants update or be reminded of archiving decisions over time, rather than one-off consent-form checkboxes.
