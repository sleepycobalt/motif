## I-03 — Quantitative quality standards (reproducibility, replicability) are seen as ill-fitted to qualitative epistemology, though an agency researcher argues the resulting lack of transparency lowers qualitative research's status

**Evidence:** bruce:0021, david:0058, michelle:0101

**Opportunity:** Funders and data services should publish qualitative-specific quality/openness criteria (rigour, transparency, documented interpretive process, and validation practices) rather than either requiring reproducibility metrics designed for quantitative data or leaving validation practice under-specified.

## I-06 — Interpretive pluralism is reframed as a feature by academic researchers, but disputed by an agency researcher as a source of credibility problems
**Claim:** Researchers in academic settings (bruce, david, michelle) explicitly argue that different readers will legitimately interpret the same qualitative data differently, treating this pluralism as valuable rather than a quality failure to be minimized; Stephen expresses similar openness but frames it as a hedged philosophical possibility rather than realized practice.  
**Confidence:** medium  
**Sources:** bruce, david, michelle, stephen  
**Evidence:** bruce:0035, stephen:0043, david:0017, michelle:0058  
**Counter-evidence:** penni:0031 — Penni, an agency researcher working across government and charity clients, argues that qualitative data's interpretive opacity and lack of shared analytic transparency contribute to it being ranked lower in a hierarchy of research methods, directly complicating the 'pluralism as a feature' framing.  
**Opportunity:** Design repository submission fields that invite and preserve the original researcher's interpretive framing ('workings out') explicitly as one reading among possible others, rather than presenting deposited data as a neutral, singular record.
