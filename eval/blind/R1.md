## I-01 — Metadata cannot substitute for lived context of data collection
**Claim:** Researchers across disciplines argue that no amount of metadata can convey the political, organisational, and temporal context that shaped qualitative data, making decontextualised re-use risky or shallow.  
**Confidence:** medium  
**Sources:** michelle, stephen, bruce  
**Evidence:** michelle:0028, michelle:0032, stephen:0022, bruce:0023  
**Counter-evidence:** david:0010 — David treats depositing his analytic 'workings out' as sufficient transparency, suggesting some researchers see context-loss as mitigable through methodological documentation rather than an absolute barrier.  
**Opportunity:** Design repository deposit templates with a mandatory free-text 'contextual note' (political/organisational/temporal circumstances) rather than only structured demographic metadata fields.

## I-04 — Widespread lack of awareness of how to make qualitative data open
**Claim:** Regardless of seniority or sector, participants report that they were never trained on qualitative open data practices and do not know who to approach, leaving guidance feeling abstract or absent.  
**Confidence:** high  
**Sources:** michelle, david, bruce, stephen, penni  
**Evidence:** michelle:0060, david:0022, bruce:0031, stephen:0051, penni:0104  
**Counter-evidence:** michelle:0081 — When researchers do actively seek support (e.g. library RDM services), they report it as excellent, suggesting the gap is discoverability/awareness rather than absence of support.  
**Opportunity:** Create a single, well-publicised 'qualitative open data' contact point/triage service per institution, modelled on the library RDM service Michelle praised, rather than leaving researchers to locate scattered guidance.
