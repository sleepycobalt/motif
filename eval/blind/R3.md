## I-01 — Lived proximity to data production cannot be fully replaced by documentation
**Claim:** Michelle and Stephen, working in very different fields, argue that meaningful understanding of qualitative data depends on tacit knowledge gained through direct involvement in its political, organizational, and relational context of production, and that this cannot be fully substituted by documentation such as metadata, field notes, or transcripts alone.  
**Confidence:** medium  
**Sources:** michelle, stephen  
**Evidence:** michelle:0028, stephen:0022  
**Counter-evidence:** michelle:0075, david:0012, david:0014 — Michelle's own later comment suggests richly narrated contextual metadata might partly bridge the gap, and David explicitly argues that sharing transcripts and full analytic workings with transparent documentation can make interpretive research usable and valuable to others, directly contesting the claim that lived proximity is irreplaceable.  
**Opportunity:** Develop flexible, narrative 'read-me' style context files (co-designed with depositing researchers) as a required companion to archived qualitative data, rather than relying on standardized metadata fields alone.

**Evidence:** stephen:0017, stephen:0018  
**Counter-evidence:** stephen:0038 — Stephen notes the barrier is sometimes negotiable: some companies accept anonymized publication without legal review, though this is an exception rather than a general solution.

**Opportunity:** Provide RDM/UKDA services with sector-specific de-identification checklists (e.g., generalizing place, sector, and role identifiers, not just names) and explicit risk-tiering rather than treating anonymization as binary.
