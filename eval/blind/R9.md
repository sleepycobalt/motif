## I-01 — Metadata can't substitute for lived context
**Claim:** Researchers argue that no amount of standard metadata can convey the organizational, political, and temporal context that gives qualitative accounts their meaning, making decontextualized reuse a distortion rather than a neutral gain.  
**Confidence:** medium  
**Sources:** michelle, stephen  
**Evidence:** michelle:0028, stephen:0022  
**Counter-evidence:** bruce:0035 — Bruce reframes divergent, context-poor readings as a legitimate feature of hermeneutic interpretation rather than a fatal loss of meaning.  
**Opportunity:** Require a structured 'context readme' (organizational, political, funding conditions at time of collection) as a mandatory companion artifact in deposit workflows, not an optional metadata field.

## I-12 — Some data may never be safely open
**Claim:** For politically sensitive topics, vulnerable populations, or small identifiable groups, researchers describe scenarios where no amount of anonymization makes open deposit safe, and participant refusal or risk of exposure should override any general openness expectation.  
**Confidence:** medium  
**Sources:** bruce, penni, stephen  
**Evidence:** penni:0031, bruce:0023, stephen:0032, bruce:0041  
**Counter-evidence:** none  
**Opportunity:** Create a formal 'non-archivable' consent and ethics-approval category so researchers and review boards can document, case by case, when a dataset is unsuitable for open deposit rather than defaulting to ad hoc judgment calls.
